---
title: "Meta reviews its 2030 net-zero deadline as AI expansion drives emissions higher"
dek: "Meta's net emissions rose 45% in 2025. Its sustainability report says the company is reassessing its climate timetable as infrastructure demand outpaces clean-energy deployment."
---

Meta put its 2030 net-zero deadline under review on November 19, 2026, publishing a sustainability report that shows net greenhouse gas emissions rose 45% in 2025 to 11.8 million metric tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent.[^1][^2]
The review covers the timetable for emissions across its value chain, extending beyond the electricity used to run its data centers.[^2]

Rachel Peterson, vice president of data centers, and Blair Swedeen, global head of net zero and sustainability, said the reassessment includes "the 2030 timeframe for our net zero emissions goal".[^2]
The executives said their long-term ambitions remain unchanged, while their operating environment has changed.[^2]

## Equipment and construction dominate the footprint

Meta's report identifies purchased goods and services and capital goods as the main drivers of the annual emissions increase.[^2]
AFP describes the value chain as including equipment, machinery, buildings and facilities, explaining why the climate commitment reaches into the infrastructure Meta buys.[^4]

Trellis reported on November 19, 2026, that emissions from purchased goods and services increased 36% in 2025, while capital-goods emissions rose 58%.[^3]
It also reported that Meta's indirect emissions were more than 105% above their 2021 level, against a supporting target to keep them from exceeding that baseline by 2030.[^3]

## Clean-energy contracts meet a slower grid

In its launch announcement, Meta said it matched 100% of its annual electricity use with clean and renewable energy for the sixth consecutive year in 2025.[^1]
It contracted more than 10 gigawatts of capacity during that year, bringing its total to more than 30 gigawatts across seven countries and 28 US states.[^1]

Those purchases sit alongside constraints on deploying new infrastructure: Peterson and Swedeen cited lengthy grid-connection queues, permitting delays and supply chains for lower-carbon materials that are still developing.[^2]
Trellis reported that Meta's electricity consumption rose 35% in 2025 as new data centers came online.[^3]

## A deadline review with the ambition retained

AFP explains that reaching net zero entails cutting emissions as far as possible and buying carbon-removal credits for emissions that remain.[^4]
Meta says it is continuing to invest and partner on its pathway while reconsidering the sequence and timing of its milestones.[^2]

Trellis distinguishes Meta from Amazon, Google and Microsoft: all reported rising emissions, but Meta was the first of those companies to publicly say it would reconsider its timelines.[^3]
Its executives have not set a date for announcing the results of that review, Trellis reported.[^3]
