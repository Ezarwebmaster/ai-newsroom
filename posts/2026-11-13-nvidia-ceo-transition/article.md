Nvidia's board of directors announced on November 12, 2026, that co-founder Jensen Huang has resigned as president, chief executive officer and a member of the board, "effective immediately".[^1][^2]
The exit ends the 33-year tenure of the executive who co-founded the chipmaker in 1993 and led its rise into a roughly $4 trillion supplier of artificial intelligence processors.[^1][^2][^6]
Executive Vice President and Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress will serve as interim CEO while retaining her finance role until the board names a permanent successor from internal and external candidates.[^1][^2]
The board also appointed Harvey C. Jones, a venture investor and former Synopsys chief executive with over 30 years in technology, as Nvidia's first-ever chair of the board.[^1][^2]

## A 13-year finance chief and a first board chair

Kress, 59, joined Nvidia as CFO in September 2013 after nearly 25 years in finance roles at companies including Cisco, Microsoft and Texas Instruments.[^1][^2][^6]
Whereas Huang oversaw about 60 direct reports himself, The Guardian noted, Jones said Kress has "a deep understanding of our business, customers, operations and financial position".[^1][^2][^6]
Jones added that the board expects Nvidia's "operations, customer commitments and product roadmap to continue without interruption".[^1][^2]
On November 12, 2026, Kress said she and the leadership team are "committed to building on the extraordinary foundation Jensen created over the last 33 years", while the board thanked Huang for his "immeasurable contributions to the company and the industry".[^1][^2][^7]

## Admitted affair, intelligence inquiries and denials

While Nvidia's release gave no reason for the departure, the Associated Press, The Guardian and Forbes reported that it came days after Huang admitted to an extramarital affair and after reports alleged additional affairs with Nvidia employees.[^1][^2][^5][^6][^7]
In a message to employees on November 8, 2026, Huang wrote, "I was unfaithful to my wife," adding, "I take full responsibility for what I did and the hurt I caused, and I accept the criticism that comes with it."[^4]
According to the Associated Press and Forbes, U.S. intelligence officials suspected that the woman outside Nvidia, a former executive at Singapore-based Megaspeed International, was acting as an agent for Beijing.[^5][^7]
Also on November 8, 2026, Nvidia's board announced an independent investigation overseen by independent directors and outside counsel into reporting that Huang "has been the target of a foreign intelligence effort".[^3]
The board said Huang committed to cooperating and stated it had "no reason to believe that either national security or NVIDIA secrets were compromised".[^3]
China denied attempting to ensnare Huang, the Associated Press reported, and Megaspeed stated on November 12, 2026, that it takes compliance with U.S. export control laws "extremely seriously".[^5]
The New York Times reported on November 11, 2026, that Huang also had an affair with an Nvidia executive who reported to him, a report the Associated Press said it could not independently confirm.[^5]

## Federal probes and an opening-bell drop

U.S. President Donald Trump announced on November 8, 2026, that the FBI was investigating, and House Foreign Affairs Committee Chair Brian Mast said his panel contacted Huang's team on November 11, 2026, after requesting an interview on November 5, 2026.[^5]
At the opening bell on November 12, 2026, Nvidia shares fell 14% while the Nasdaq slid 1.5%, the Associated Press reported, whereas Forbes put the opening drop at 10% and the five-day decline at 18%.[^5][^7]
