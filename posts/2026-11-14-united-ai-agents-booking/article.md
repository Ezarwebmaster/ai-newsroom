United Airlines announced on November 11, 2026, that it is restricting unauthorized third-party personal AI agents from accessing its systems to book flights or manage reservations.[^1]
Updates to United's booking, ticketing and distribution policies and its contract of carriage prohibit using AI agents to "make, modify or service a ticket, and/or purchase any service or product".[^1][^3]
The restrictions apply directly on United's website and mobile app and indirectly through travel agencies and aggregators, which AirlineGeeks reports is the first such move by a U.S. airline.[^1][^2]

AideDex, which examined United’s contract and customer help page, reports that the contract prohibits agents from buying or changing tickets even if a traveler supervises or approves the action.[^5]
The same report says the customer help page takes a broader approach to unauthorized tools that search or collect information on United’s site or app.[^5]
<!-- /add -->

## MileagePlus logins, federal flight rules and prior bookings

United said it is restricting unauthorized personal AI agents to protect customer data and accounts.[^1]
Sharing credentials with external assistants exposes sensitive information and leaves customers open to scams, unintended purchases, account changes and use of MileagePlus miles, the airline said.[^1][^2]
United also warned that automated actions could violate Federal Aviation Administration and Department of Transportation regulations, pointing to the check-in requirement that passengers confirm in person that their bags contain no banned items such as explosives.[^2]
<!-- add role="checker" model="Google Gemini 4 Argon" -->
In its own release, United describes that rule as requiring "human acknowledgment that bags do not contain banned substances like explosives" during check-in, alongside updates to its booking, ticketing and distribution policies.[^1]
<!-- /add -->
United plans to update its Digital Trust Center to clarify the new restrictions, help customers protect accounts, and explain where they can get help if an agent takes an unintended action.[^1][^2]
<!-- add role="reviser" model="OpenAI GPT-6.1 Terra" -->
AideDex reports that United will not cancel agent bookings made before November 11, 2026, and advises travelers to check that those trips were ticketed correctly.[^5]
The publication did not test an agent booking, searching or checking in, so its account does not establish how the restrictions work in practice.[^5]
<!-- /add -->

## Kirby's middleman warning and rival carriers' stances

Beyond security and compliance, United has resisted letting outside AI assistants stand between the airline and travelers.[^4]
"To be honest, I don't want to create another middleman," Chief Executive Officer Scott Kirby said at an event on November 10, 2026, according to The Wall Street Journal as cited by Engadget.[^4]
United encouraged other companies to consider similar security measures, and Engadget notes that JPMorgan Chase and Amazon also block agents.[^1][^2][^4]
Other major U.S. carriers have taken different approaches, with Fast Company reporting that Delta Air Lines and American Airlines still give some AI tools booking privileges while they evaluate the landscape.[^3]
Delta told Fast Company it supports authenticated AI interactions with additional safeguards for sensitive transactions, adding that passengers remain responsible for overseeing actions and authorizing transactions taken on their behalf.[^3]
Southwest Airlines announced a ChatGPT plugin partnership in October 2026, though representatives for Southwest, JetBlue, Alaska and Frontier did not immediately respond to Fast Company's requests for comment.[^3]

## Low AI checkout volume and a 1.8% stock dip

Engadget wrote that "agentic commerce might not be taking off with regular people".[^4]
During its third-quarter earnings call, Uber disclosed that AI agents accounted for 500 Uber Eats orders and four rides out of 320 million trips and deliveries during the week of Halloween, Engadget reported.[^4]
Still, United sought to avoid incidents such as Meta's Muse leaking a user's address online, according to Engadget.[^4]
On November 11, 2026, United shares fell 1.8% while the S&P 500 Index rose 1.3%, Fast Company reported.[^3]

## What United's announcement leaves unanswered

- Which AI assistants or travel partners could later receive authorization: United bars "unauthorized, third-party personal AI agents" across its platforms and via aggregators without naming any approved external agent or certification process.[^1]
- How United detects or blocks agent activity in practice: neither the newsroom release nor the press reports specify the technical controls used on its website, mobile app or distribution channels.[^1][^2][^5]
- When the promised Digital Trust Center guidance goes live: United says it "will also update" the center to explain the restrictions and where travelers can seek help, without giving a date.[^1][^2]
- Where rival carriers stand on third-party agents: American Airlines did not detail which AI tools hold booking privileges on its systems, and Southwest, JetBlue, Alaska and Frontier had not answered Fast Company's questions at publication.[^3]
