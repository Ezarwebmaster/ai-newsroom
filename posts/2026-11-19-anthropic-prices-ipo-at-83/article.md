title: "Anthropic prices IPO at $83 a share, raising up to $91.6 billion at a $1.5 trillion valuation"

---

Surpassing the $70 to $78 range set during its roadshow, Anthropic priced its initial public offering at $83.00 per share on November 18, 2026.[^1][^2][^3]
The pricing values the artificial intelligence company at about $1.5 trillion fully diluted, or $1.1 trillion non-diluted, making it the second most valuable initial public offering in history behind SpaceX's $1.77 trillion listing in June 2026.[^2][^3][^5]
<!-- add role="reviser" model="Google Gemini 4 Argon" -->
With OpenAI holding off on a listing until at least 2027, Fortune noted that Anthropic is the first pure-play frontier AI model developer to go public, ranking 12th globally by market value behind Tesla.[^2][^4]
<!-- /add -->
The Securities and Exchange Commission declared the company's registration statement effective on November 18, 2026.[^1]

## Expanded float tops SpaceX with $81 billion in gross proceeds

Anthropic increased the size of the offering to 980,000,000 Class A shares, up from the 825 million shares initially planned.[^1][^2]
<!-- add role="reviser" model="Google Gemini 4 Argon" -->
The roughly 19% increase followed roadshow demand reaching several times the shares offered, Reuters reported via CNA, with Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley leading a 22-bank underwriting syndicate.[^5]
<!-- /add -->
The company is selling 956,351,354 shares itself, while selling stockholders are offering 23,648,646 shares.[^1]
Anthropic will not receive any proceeds from the shares sold by existing stockholders, which include CEO Dario Amodei, President Daniela Amodei and Chief Science Officer Jared Kaplan cashing in small portions of equity.[^1][^2][^3]
Gross proceeds of $81.3 billion place the initial offering ahead of SpaceX's $75 billion market debut in June 2026, though SpaceX's total haul later reached $85.7 billion after its underwriters exercised an overallotment option.[^2][^3][^4][^5]
Fortune reports that Anthropic will raise approximately $79.4 billion from its own shares, and up to $91.6 billion if underwriters exercise their option.[^2]
Underwriters hold a 30-day option to purchase up to an additional 147,000,000 Class A shares from the company.[^1]
In addition to the public offering, seven strategic partners including Google and Nvidia have agreed to purchase $26 billion of non-voting stock at the $83 price.[^2][^4]
<!-- add role="reviser" model="Google Gemini 4 Argon" -->
Combined with the private placement, total capital raised reaches $107.3 billion, or up to roughly $118 billion for Anthropic if the overallotment option is exercised.[^2][^4]
<!-- /add -->

## Trading set for Nasdaq as lockups and founder sales take effect

Anthropic's Class A stock is expected to begin trading on the Nasdaq Global Select Market on November 19, 2026 under the symbol “ANT.”[^1]
The offering is scheduled to close on November 20, 2026, subject to customary closing conditions.[^1]
Dan Ives of Yorkville Ives & Co. told the Associated Press that the pricing reflects strong demand among investors after leadership turmoil at Nvidia.[^3]
<!-- add role="reviser" model="Google Gemini 4 Argon" -->
Over the preceding month, the Nasdaq Composite shed nearly 7.6% of its value, led by a drop of more than 30% in Nvidia's stock after CEO Jensen Huang stepped down, Al Jazeera reported.[^4]
<!-- /add -->
Despite the large initial valuation, Morningstar analyst Malik Ahmed Khan said he believed Anthropic’s fair value is actually closer to $92 per share, citing expected growth in enterprise AI adoption.[^2]
<!-- add role="reviser" model="Google Gemini 4 Argon" -->
Khan cautioned on November 18, 2026, however, that Anthropic must manage "the enormous cost of developing and running its models" amid "considerable uncertainty around how the competitive landscape and economics of generative AI will evolve."[^2]
According to prospectus figures and data cited by Reuters, Anthropic had 11,900 customers generating at least $100,000 in trailing 12-month revenue as of September 30, 2026, up from more than 1,500 at the end of 2025, though corporate expense platform Ramp tracked Anthropic's Claude Fable at about 8% of enterprise AI spending in September 2026 behind OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra at 13%.[^5]
Peter Singlehurst, head of private companies at Baillie Gifford, which invested roughly $1.5 billion in Anthropic before the IPO, told Reuters that his firm's thesis is that "the vast amount of value will accrue to whoever is at the frontier."[^5]
<!-- /add -->

## Infrastructure commitments reach $900 billion amid sector debt

The capital infusion arrives as Anthropic faces massive operational costs to develop and train frontier artificial intelligence systems.[^2]
<!-- add role="reviser" model="Google Gemini 4 Argon" -->
Anthropic spent nearly $9 billion on computing capacity and infrastructure in the first half of 2026, nearly four times its spending a year earlier, plus $5 billion in third-quarter computing costs, Reuters reported.[^5]
<!-- /add -->
Anthropic has already signed commercial leases and agreements committing it to spend $900 billion on computing power and infrastructure over the coming years.[^3]
Those commitments include $70 billion scheduled to be spent within the next 12 months.[^3]
<!-- add role="reviser" model="Google Gemini 4 Argon" -->
In its prospectus, Anthropic warned that some computing partners rely on third-party financing to buy chips and build capacity, and that if such support becomes unavailable or costlier, "contracted capacity may be delayed, repriced, reduced, or not delivered."[^5]
<!-- /add -->
The Associated Press reported that Anthropic carries $6.2 billion in debt, which company filings describe as substantial.[^3]
Although the company remains unprofitable under standard accounting rules, public listing proceeds provide substantial funding without taking on additional high-interest loans.[^3]
