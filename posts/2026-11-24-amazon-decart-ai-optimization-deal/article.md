Amazon announced on November 23, 2026 that it signed an agreement to buy the proprietary technology behind the Decart Optimization Stack (DOS) and bring co-founders Dean Leitersdorf, Moshe Shalev and Orian Leitersdorf, along with their AI researchers and engineers, into the company.[^1]
Amazon did not disclose financial terms, but Israeli business outlets Calcalist and Globes reported the transaction at about $7.5 billion, with Calcalist describing a mix of cash, stock and deferred installments tied to performance and Globes reporting multi-year targets over five years.[^2][^3][^4]
Completion of the agreement remains subject to customary closing conditions, including regulatory clearance.[^1]

## Closing the software gap for external Trainium sales

Decart built its public profile on real-time interactive world models such as Oasis 3 and live video tools like Lucy, while its underlying DOS software tunes AI workloads to run across chips from Nvidia, Amazon and Google.[^2][^5]
Amazon is acquiring the optimization technology and engineering team rather than Decart's consumer AI models, GeekWire reported, citing Bloomberg.[^2]
The software will be integrated through Amazon's Annapurna Labs chip division to improve efficiency across its custom Trainium and Inferentia processors, according to Globes.[^4]
Amazon has used Trainium inside its own data centers and plans to sell the chips directly to external customers, a strategy CEO Andy Jassy highlighted on the company's October 2026 earnings call, saying, "We believe we're going to have a very significant business selling chips externally."[^2][^5]

The two companies have collaborated since 2025, when Decart became an early Trainium3 user and Amazon said in December 2025 that Decart ran real-time video four times faster on Trainium at half the cost of GPUs.[^1][^2]
"Every leap forward in AI starts with getting more out of every chip," said Peter DeSantis, Amazon's senior vice president of foundational AI models, custom silicon and quantum computing, adding, "More efficient compute means better training, better reinforcement learning, faster inference, and better models for customers at a lower cost."[^1]
Decart co-founder and CEO Dean Leitersdorf said, "Joining Amazon gives our team the scale, the customers, and the infrastructure to pursue that mission faster than we could on our own."[^1]

## Five-year founder retention and Annapurna Labs' role

Globes reported that Annapurna Labs co-founder Nafea Bshara, who leads engineering for Amazon's chip division, led negotiations for Amazon.[^4]
Amazon acquired Annapurna Labs, also founded in Israel, in 2015 for a reported $350 million to $375 million, and the unit went on to design Trainium and Amazon's other custom silicon.[^2][^4]
Under the reported terms, Decart's three co-founders hold about 64% of the startup's shares—worth roughly $4.8 billion collectively—and have committed to remain at Amazon for five years, with a significant portion of the payout deferred to the later years.[^3][^4]

## Collapsed talks with Nvidia and Anthropic

The Amazon agreement closes months of acquisition talks that began after Calcalist reported on August 10, 2026 that Decart was fielding buyer interest.[^3]
Nvidia, which invested in Decart's $300 million funding round in May 2026, held early acquisition discussions before the founders pursued a roughly $6 billion deal with Anthropic.[^2][^3]
Anthropic completed due diligence and exchanged draft agreements before withdrawing in early September 2026, and Nvidia declined to reopen talks afterward, clearing the way for Amazon.[^3]
