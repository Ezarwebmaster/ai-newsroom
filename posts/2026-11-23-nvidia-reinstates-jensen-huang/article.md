Eleven days after Jensen Huang stepped down, Nvidia announced on November 23, 2026 that its board voted unanimously to reinstate the co-founder as president, chief executive officer and director, "effective immediately".[^1][^4]
A Form 8-K filed on November 23, 2026 records that directors reinstated Huang on November 22, 2026.[^2]
Interim chief executive Colette Kress continues as executive vice president and chief financial officer, and Harvey C. Jones remains board chair.[^1][^2]
Nvidia said meetings with Huang satisfied directors on his "candor and ability to lead NVIDIA", while input from "senior levels of the United States government" left the board confident his return "poses no national security risk".[^1]

TIME noted that the board acted before completing its independent investigation and did not name the government officials it consulted or detail the information they provided.[^4]

## Reversing the November 17 stance while keeping compliance probes

The vote reverses the stance Jones took on Nvidia's earnings call on November 17, 2026, when he said directors did "not intend to reinstate Huang" after hiring Heidrick & Struggles to find a successor, TIME and TechCrunch reported.[^4][^5]
<!-- add role="reviser" model="Google Gemini 4 Argon" -->
Arm Holdings chief executive Rene Haas, who had been approached to succeed Huang, signed a new contract keeping him in his current role until 2033, The Guardian reported.[^7]
<!-- /add -->
In Nvidia's November 23, 2026 release, Jones said Huang's return serves "the best interests of our stockholders, employees, partners and customers", and Huang said he returned with "the greatest humility and renewed commitment".[^1]
<!-- add role="reviser" model="Google Gemini 4 Argon" -->
Huang added that he was "honored to return to NVIDIA and lead one of the most consequential technology companies in the world at the beginning of a new industrial revolution".[^1]
<!-- /add -->
Huang resigned on November 12, 2026 after acknowledging an extramarital affair and amid reports of other extramarital relationships, TIME and the Los Angeles Times reported.[^3][^4]
The Los Angeles Times, TIME and TechCrunch reported that U.S. intelligence suspected the woman outside Nvidia, a former Megaspeed executive, of spying for China, though TIME noted intelligence agencies have not publicly established she knowingly spied and Beijing denied the allegation.[^3][^4][^5]
<!-- add role="reviser" model="Google Gemini 4 Argon" -->
On November 23, 2026, the U.S. government added Singapore-based Megaspeed to a federal trade blacklist barring U.S. companies from selling it chips or advanced technology, The Guardian reported.[^7]
<!-- /add -->
Nvidia said its independent committee's inquiry into reported foreign intelligence targeting "will proceed to conclusion" with Huang's cooperation, alongside its National Security and Export Control Committee, a chief compliance officer search and cooperation with government inquiries.[^1]
<!-- add role="reviser" model="Google Gemini 4 Argon" -->
Nvidia stated that both board committees consist solely of independent directors and are assisted by outside counsel, while the new chief compliance officer will report to the Nominating and Corporate Governance Committee.[^1]

## White House intervention and a 30% market jump

President Donald Trump told CNBC on November 23, 2026, "I convinced them" and "I made the deal" after speaking to directors "directly and indirectly" alongside Vice President JD Vance, TIME reported.[^4]
<!-- add role="reviser" model="Google Gemini 4 Argon" -->
Trump also told CNBC that he was "represented in the board meeting", that the United States had to "protect our geniuses" and that Chinese President Xi Jinping had praised Huang at the APEC summit in Shenzhen, Al Jazeera and The Guardian reported.[^6][^7]
<!-- /add -->
On Truth Social on November 23, 2026, Trump called the move "correct and proper" and wrote that Attorney General Todd Blanche did "not possess evidence" of unlawful conduct by Huang, Al Jazeera and TIME reported.[^4][^6]
An online petition backing Huang had passed 180,000 signatures, including Elon Musk, TIME and Al Jazeera reported, while TechCrunch noted that Musk and other tech executives signed it.[^4][^5][^6]
<!-- add role="reviser" model="Google Gemini 4 Argon" -->
Signatories also included Replit CEO Amjad Masad, Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney, Atoms founder Travis Kalanick and Y Combinator CEO Garry Tan, TechCrunch reported, and Musk wrote on X on November 23, 2026 that "The mistake has been corrected", according to the Los Angeles Times.[^3][^5]
<!-- /add -->
Nvidia shares opened 30% higher on November 23, 2026 and traded at $159.95 per share, up 27%, at 9:51 a.m. ET, TechCrunch reported, while Al Jazeera reported an opening jump above 33% as the Nasdaq rose 6.7% and the S&P 500 gained 4.6% in early trading.[^5][^6]
<!-- add role="reviser" model="Google Gemini 4 Argon" -->
The Guardian reported that Nvidia's stock had dropped by a quarter after Huang's November 12, 2026 resignation and that the November 23, 2026 rally added over $1 trillion (£760 billion) in market value, while Morningstar director Lindsey Stewart told TIME that reinstating Huang before finishing the inquiry "is going to add to those corporate governance questions that investors have".[^4][^7]

## Bipartisan skepticism and December hearings in Congress

Lawmakers in both parties questioned the reversal on November 23, 2026, with Republican Senators John Cornyn and John Curtis asking what basis supported the security finding and Democratic Senator Elizabeth Warren writing that "Huang and NVIDIA still owe Congress answers", the Los Angeles Times, TIME and Al Jazeera reported.[^3][^4][^6]
<!-- add role="reviser" model="Google Gemini 4 Argon" -->
Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer said "The Trump Administration's rush to reinstate Jensen Huang underscores the need for Congress to act" and pledged Democrats would "wield their full power" to investigate Huang, Nvidia and the administration, while Representative Greg Casar said "Jensen Huang must testify under oath", the Los Angeles Times and TIME reported.[^3][^4]
<!-- /add -->
Huang is scheduled for closed-door sessions on December 1, 2026 with three House committees and a December 3, 2026 Senate Banking hearing on export controls, the Los Angeles Times and TIME reported.[^3][^4]
California Attorney General Rob Bonta called the return "wrong, and it's shocking" on November 23, 2026 as his office continues investigating Nvidia, alongside ongoing SEC and Justice Department inquiries noted by Al Jazeera.[^3][^6]

## No timetable for the board inquiry, compliance hire or security disclosures

- No deadline for the board's independent inquiry: Nvidia stated the committee's investigation into reported foreign intelligence targeting will proceed to conclusion without setting a completion date or saying whether findings will be made public.[^1]
- No named government officials or security assessment: Nvidia's November 23, 2026 release and Form 8-K do not identify the senior U.S. officials consulted or disclose the basis for concluding Huang's return poses no national security risk.[^1][^2][^4]
- No hire date or remit details for the chief compliance officer: Nvidia announced only that it is commencing a search for the new role, without specifying a hiring timetable or staffing budget.[^1]
- No disclosed terms for Huang's return or government probe timelines: Nvidia's Form 8-K does not detail any changes to Huang's compensation, and the SEC, Justice Department and California inquiries carry no announced completion dates.[^2][^3][^6]
