European Union leaders held their first strategic discussion on artificial intelligence on November 13, 2026, at an informal European Council meeting in Dublin, the Council said.[^1]
The debate had two parts: how AI can raise European competitiveness, and its "wider impact on society, security and trust".[^1]
No conclusions were expected: Euronews noted beforehand that the informal format leaves "no political statements to adopt at the end".[^2]

## Mensch asks for speed, Costa points to the budget

Arthur Mensch, chief executive of France's Mistral AI, joined the first session, on competitiveness and what the Council calls AI sovereignty.[^1]
"Europe has all the technology and the talent to lead the AI race," he told the leaders, according to Agence France-Presse.[^4]
He added that Europe now needs "speed, financing and a strong coordination between member states", and said decisions taken over the next two years would shape the industry for the next decade.[^4]

The Council lists three subjects for that session: public and private investment and infrastructure, European capabilities and supply dependencies, and clean, affordable energy.[^1]
European Council President António Costa said that catching up with global peers in AI "will require significant additional funding", and that the next EU long-term budget, through a new European Competitiveness Fund, "will play a decisive role".[^1]
The Council's AI summary gives no funding amount or agreed allocation.[^1]

In her November 13, 2026, closing statement, Commission President Ursula von der Leyen estimated that technological sovereignty would require at least €500 billion in investment.[^5]
She called for private capital and support from the next EU budget, and promised a financing proposal; her statement set out estimated needs rather than an approved spending allocation.[^5]

## Chip targets and gigafactories behind schedule

The Chips Act looks likely to fall short of its goal of doubling the bloc's share of the global chip market to 20% by 2030, Reuters wrote before the meeting.[^3]
A plan for seven AI gigafactories "has yet to fully take off", it added, because EU countries are fighting over competitiveness spending in the 2028-2034 budget.[^3]
<!-- add role="reviser" model="OpenAI GPT-6 Astra" -->
In her closing statement, von der Leyen said around €30 billion was being mobilised for the seven planned gigafactories.[^5]
<!-- /add -->
Euronews reported that the EU is also advancing rules to ensure that public contracts in sensitive use cases go only to European companies.[^2]

## Hinton's session: rights, elections and labour markets

Geoffrey Hinton, the Nobel laureate who has estimated a 10% to 20% risk that AI could cause human extinction, joined the second session.[^1][^2]
Leaders discussed fundamental rights, including children's rights, the integrity of democratic processes, labour markets, market concentration and cybersecurity, the Council says.[^1]
They also exchanged views on the need for "global safeguards that uphold democratic values and fundamental rights" for advanced AI.[^1]
Costa cited the AI Act, noting that the EU "is the first region in the world to establish an AI governance framework".[^1]
Ahead of the summit, Estonia and Finland called in a letter for EU-level arrangements to respond to serious AI incidents, AFP reported.[^4]
EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas warned of a "dark side of AI", pointing to AI-equipped drones in Ukraine.[^4]

## A summit that shared the day with Ukraine

The AI agenda competed for attention: Euronews wrote that the question of who should speak for the EU in the Ukraine peace talks "has taken over" the summit.[^2]

Costa said leaders would return to AI at their meeting in Alden Biesen on February 5, 2027.[^6]

Costa also announced that AI and its impact on jobs will be the central theme of the 2027 social summit, which marks the 10th anniversary of the European Pillar of Social Rights.[^1][^6]
Money comes back sooner, at a special European Council on November 26 and 27, 2026, where Ukraine financing and the EU budget "will take centre stage", according to Euronews.[^2]
