Alphabet's AI drug discovery company Isomorphic Labs announced on November 17, 2026, that it has raised $5 billion (£3.8 billion) in an oversubscribed Series C round.[^1][^2][^5]
Greenoaks and Sequoia Capital co-led the financing, joined by new investor Andreessen Horowitz and continued investment from Alphabet.[^1][^2]
All existing backers also participated, including Thrive Capital, MGX, GV, CapitalG, Temasek and the UK Sovereign AI Fund.[^1]
Proceeds will fund Isomorphic Labs' drug design engine IsoDDE, expand its therapeutic pipeline and build translational and clinical infrastructure as the company prepares to move its first candidates into human trials.[^1][^2]

## Greenoaks, Sequoia and Alphabet double down six months after Series B

The Series C follows a $2.1 billion Series B round in May 2026 and lifts Isomorphic Labs' total disclosed external funding since 2025 to approximately $7.7 billion.[^3][^5]
Isomorphic Labs did not disclose a valuation for the new round.[^3][^5]
Bloomberg reported in November 2026 that the lab sought funding at a $50 billion valuation, and City A.M. noted October 2026 reports of up to $50 billion compared with roughly $17.5 billion previously.[^3][^5]
It is Isomorphic Labs' first financing since founder and chief executive Sir Demis Hassabis stepped away from running Google DeepMind to become DeepMind's chairman and focus on drug discovery, TechCrunch reported.[^3]
Founded in 2021 out of DeepMind, Isomorphic Labs is headquartered in London with offices in Cambridge, Massachusetts, and Lausanne, Switzerland, and is led by Hassabis and president Max Jaderberg.[^2][^3]
UK AI minister Kanishka Narayan welcomed the round, calling it "one of the biggest Series C rounds in UK tech history" and highlighting the Sovereign AI Fund's follow-on backing.[^5]

## How IsoDDE skips X-ray protein scans

Isomorphic Labs says IsoDDE unifies foundational simulation AI models across therapeutic areas and drug modalities to explore chemical spaces and design candidate molecules.[^1][^2]
According to SiliconANGLE, multiple pharmaceutical companies already use IsoDDE in drug discovery programs.[^4]
The software speeds up locating binding pockets on target proteins or helps researchers design compounds that prompt a protein to form a pocket when none exists, Isomorphic Labs told SiliconANGLE.[^4]
Traditional FEP algorithms require scientists to freeze a target protein in a crystal and scan it with X-rays to build a 3D model, a step Isomorphic Labs says IsoDDE skips while claiming higher-quality output than those algorithms.[^4]
Hassabis said the engine is "generating molecules with unprecedented precision and speed," while Jaderberg said teams are making repeatable weekly advances and "routinely solving complex biological problems that have stalled traditional research for decades."[^2][^4]

## Preclinical results, pharma deals and no human trials yet

Isomorphic Labs said its models have produced positive preclinical results in internal oncology and immunology programs as well as partnered work.[^2]
Its pharmaceutical partners include Eli Lilly, Novartis and Johnson & Johnson.[^3][^5]
Despite raising billions, Isomorphic Labs has not yet started human clinical trials for its own drug candidates, City A.M. reported.[^5]
City A.M. reported that the company remains in late-stage preclinical development as it builds global bioengineering and clinical infrastructure for trial entry.[^2][^5]
