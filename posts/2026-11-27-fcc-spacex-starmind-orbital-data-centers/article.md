---
title: "FCC approves SpaceX conditional license for 1 million orbital AI data center satellites"
dek: "The Federal Communications Commission granted conditional authority on November 25, 2026 for SpaceX's Starmind constellation, setting deployment milestones through 2040 while deferring approval for satellites operating above 600 kilometers."
---

On November 25, 2026, the Federal Communications Commission granted conditional approval to SpaceX to deploy and operate an orbital AI data center constellation of up to 1 million satellites known as Starmind.[^1][^2]
The regulatory authorization from the agency's Space Bureau marks the first federal license permitting data center infrastructure to operate in low-Earth orbit.[^1][^4]
Under the terms of the order, primary inter-satellite communications will rely on optical laser links, while the agency authorized backup and emergency telemetry, tracking, and command operations in the Ka-band radio frequencies on an unprotected, non-interference basis.[^1]

## Orbital compute shells and backup radio authorization

The approved constellation plans to place up to 1,000,000 non-geostationary satellites across orbital shells at altitudes ranging from 500 to 1,002 kilometers.[^1]
SpaceX sought regulatory permission to harness continuous solar energy in space to process machine learning workloads for users back on Earth.[^2][^4]
To accommodate the system, the commission granted a waiver of the U.S. Table of Frequency Allocations for backup telemetry in the 18.8 to 19.3 GHz space-to-Earth and 28.6 to 29.1 GHz Earth-to-space bands.[^1]
The regulator determined that the authorization serves the public interest by allowing the company "to pioneer emergent technology that advances American leadership in space and artificial intelligence."[^1]

## Deployment schedule across fourteen years to 2040

Rather than applying standard satellite milestone rules, the commission granted a waiver applying the new Part 100 deployment schedule adopted in its space modernization proceeding.[^1][^3]
The license requires SpaceX to place its first operational Starmind satellite into orbit no later than November 2033.[^1][^3]
SpaceX must subsequently deploy 10 percent of the authorized constellation, representing 100,000 satellites, no later than November 2035.[^1][^3]
The schedule mandates that 50 percent of the constellation, or 500,000 satellites, must be operational by November 2038, with full constellation deployment required by November 2040.[^1][^3]
The agency also waived standard surety bond requirements, citing the non-interference status of the emergency frequencies.[^1]

## Petitions over orbital debris and deferred high-altitude approval

The authorization was issued despite petitions to deny filed by Amazon's Kuiper Systems, Viasat, and the American Astronomical Society.[^1][^2]
Competitors and astronomers argued that 1 million satellites would increase orbital congestion, disrupt ground telescopes, and create hazardous space debris.[^1][^2][^4]
The commission dismissed environmental objections and astronomical disruption claims, finding that environmental reviews under the National Environmental Policy Act do not extend to outer space operations and that agency rules do not govern optical astronomy.[^1][^2]
The agency approved orbital debris mitigation plans only for satellites flying below 600 kilometers, where atmospheric drag ensures that retired spacecraft fully demise upon reentry.[^1][^3]
For satellites planned above 600 kilometers, the commission deferred approval because SpaceX sought permission to maneuver decommissioned satellites into graveyard orbits without submitting sufficient post-mission disposal details.[^1][^3]
